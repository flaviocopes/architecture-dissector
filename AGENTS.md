# Blueprint

A SwiftUI macOS app, plus a `blueprint` command-line tool, that draws the architecture of the apps you track, what people do with them and the data they store, plays short explainers about them, and shows how all of it changed between versions. Agents write the architectures with the command; the app never generates them. It has no dependencies, and its only automated check is `scripts/fuzz-layout.sh`.

The app and the command share the files in `Blueprint/Core`, which only import Foundation:

- `Models.swift`: `Architecture` (summary, walkthrough steps, groups, nodes with their role and details, edges, flows: every workflow of the app, as steps in lanes with optional branches, each with its actor (`FlowActor`: user, owner, agent or the app on its own) and its area, entities: what it stores, each in a store component, with fields that can be a key or point to another entity, and explainers: short videos as scenes that each show components, flow steps or entities, with a caption), `NodeKind`, `EdgeKind`, `StepKind`, `Snapshot` (an architecture saved at a time, commit and optional version) and `TrackedApp`.
- `Library.swift`: `Paths` and everything on disk in `~/Library/Application Support/Blueprint`: tracking folders, saving the current architecture with its manifest, versions, past versions, and finding an app by id, name or folder.
- `Git.swift`: read-only git commands, and the manifest: the content hash of every file git doesn't ignore.
- `Status.swift`: `AppStatus`, which compares the saved manifest with a fresh one to tell which files and components changed since the last save.
- `Diff.swift`: `ArchitectureDiff`, which compares two architectures by node id, edge ends, and entity id with fields by name, and merges them so removed parts can still be drawn, removed fields included, in their old place.
- `Validation.swift`: decoding with readable errors, normalizing, and the checks `blueprint set` runs.

App only:

- `AppStore.swift`: the observable state: tracked apps, the version shown, the comparison, the walkthrough step, the canvas mode (Architecture, Flows, Data or Explainers) and what's selected in each, the explainer slide and whether it plays, the file watchers, the status checks, and `blueprint://` URLs.
- `Layout.swift`: `DiagramLayout`. Each group is a box. Boxes get columns from a longest-path ranking, then move between columns while that makes connections shorter overall (a connection inside a column or pointing back costs more than one spanning columns). Inside a box, cards stack in the order their connections flow. Sweeps line boxes and cards up with their neighbors. The `Router` turns connections into curves: neighbors get one curve, longer ones run through the gaps between the boxes of each column they cross, bundled side by side, and connections within a column become nested arcs on its quieter side. Labels take the first spot along their connection that covers no card or other label, or show only while highlighted.
- `DiagramCanvas.swift`: `ZoomableCanvas`, the pannable and zoomable grid both canvases use (camera, minimap, zoom controls, the scroll and pinch event monitor), and on top of it the architecture: `Diagram`, `DiagramWorld`, group boxes, animated edges and labels. Below 55% zoom the group titles move out of their boxes into `Callout`s: full names at a constant size on screen, with an arrow to the box, placed where they cover no box, card or other callout. Clicking one zooms to its group.
- `DataDiagram.swift`: the Data view: `DataLayout` (one box per store, ordered by the references between them; inside a box, entities in columns that follow their references, each card moved toward what it points to; a reference runs from its field's row to the header of its target, through the gaps between the cards of the columns it crosses), entity cards with a row per field, and the empty state.
- `ExplainerPlayer.swift`: the Explainers view. `ExplainerSlide` turns scenes into slides (an intro, then one per scene, timed by their words), `ExplainerStage` draws a slide at 1280 × 720 scaled to fit, always light: the real architecture, flow or data world with a spotlight, a camera that glides to what the slide shows, a caption, and a title card for scenes that show nothing. `ExplainerPlayer` times the slides and has the controls, and `ExplainerSound` synthesizes the whoosh, pop and chime with `AVAudioEngine`.
- `FlowDiagram.swift`: the Flows view: `FlowDiagramLayout` (steps top to bottom in the order they happen, one column per lane, branches side by side; a link down to another column turns out of the card's side, or down into the target's side, whichever crosses no card, because a curve from bottom to top gets squeezed flat in the gap between rows), step cards, lane columns, the flow picker and the empty state.
- `NodeCard.swift`, `Theme.swift` (colors and symbols per kind, diff colors, and the diagram themes: `DiagramTheme` picks Blueprint, Terminal, Minimal or ASCII, and `DiagramStyle` holds the type, ink, paper, borders and corners of every theme but Blueprint), `ContentView.swift` (split view, sidebar, drag and drop, save version sheet), `DetailView.swift` (toolbar, banners, the walkthrough card, timeline, the screen for an app no agent mapped yet).
- `Inspector.swift`: the app overview with its walkthrough steps, a component's role card, the list of changes when comparing, the status, in Flows the flows grouped by actor or by area (`AppStore.flowSections`, which the flow picker uses too) and a step's details, in Data the stores and their entities, an entity's fields and what points to it, and the data changes when comparing, and in Explainers the list of explainers with the scenes of the one playing.
- `Welcome.swift`: the first screen, shown full window while no app is tracked: `SetupGuide`, the five setup steps (install the command, install the agent skill, track an app with the drop zone and recent projects, the prompt that maps it, and the `AGENTS.md` snippet that keeps it current), which the Set Up Agents sheet shows too, and a live demo that loops through mapping, flagged changes and a version comparison of a made-up app.
- `ProjectFinder.swift`: the most recently used git repositories in `~/dev`, `~/Developer`, `~/Projects` and similar folders, for the welcome screen. `BLUEPRINT_PROJECTS_HOME` points it at another home, which `scripts/screenshot.sh` fills with made-up repositories.
- `FileWatcher.swift`, `CommandLineTool.swift` (links the bundled command into `/usr/local/bin`), `AgentSkill.swift` (copies the bundled skill to `~/.agents/skills/blueprint` and links it into the skill folders of Claude Code, Cursor and Codex), `BlueprintApp.swift` (the window, the menus, and starting the updater).
- `AppUpdater.swift`: in-app updates from GitHub Releases. It's a copy of a shared template, kept identical across apps, so don't edit it here.
- `AppIcon.icon`: the Icon Composer icon, generated by `scripts/render-icon.swift`.

Command only: `CLI/main.swift` (arguments and help), `CLI/Commands.swift`, `CLI/Guide.swift` (the instructions `blueprint guide` prints for agents), `CLI/Terminal.swift`.

## Build and run

Requirements: macOS 15 or later, Xcode 26 (the `.icon` needs it), and XcodeGen after editing `project.yml`.

```bash
scripts/build-release.sh           # universal Release build, dist/Blueprint-<version>.zip, notarized when the Developer ID is in the keychain
open build/release/Release/Blueprint.app
build/release/Release/Blueprint.app/Contents/Helpers/blueprint list
xcodegen generate                  # after editing project.yml
swift scripts/render-icon.swift    # after editing the icon
scripts/screenshot.sh              # docs/screenshot-*.png, from the architectures in scripts/demo
scripts/screenshot.sh /tmp/shots /tmp/app.json   # only that architecture, like one from blueprint show --json
scripts/fuzz-layout.sh             # 400 random architectures, 300 random flows and 300 random data schemas through the layouts, checked for overlaps
```

`project.yml` is the source of the Xcode project, so edit it and regenerate instead of changing `project.pbxproj` by hand. The version lives in its `MARKETING_VERSION`. A file the command needs goes in `Blueprint/Core`, and it can't import SwiftUI or AppKit.

## Rules

- Blueprint never writes to a tracked folder, not even its git refs. It runs read-only git commands there, with `GIT_OPTIONAL_LOCKS=0`, and writes only to its own data folder. Removing an app or a version moves its files to the Trash.
- Agents write architectures, the app only draws them. Don't add code that generates an architecture from the source.
- Versions are compared by node and entity id, and fields by name, so `blueprint guide` tells agents to keep them stable. Keep that rule when the format changes.
- When the format changes, update `CLI/Guide.swift`, the checks in `Validation.swift`, the demo files in `scripts/demo`, and the description in `skill/blueprint/SKILL.md`, which lists what agents can map. Decoding stays lenient (missing arrays are empty, unknown kinds draw as services), and validation stays strict.
- `BLUEPRINT_HOME` moves the data folder. Test the command and the app against a made-up one, never your real data: `BLUEPRINT_HOME=/tmp/blueprint-test blueprint add ~/Projects/my-app`. Launch the app with `open --env BLUEPRINT_HOME=/tmp/blueprint-test build/release/Release/Blueprint.app`.
- `scripts/screenshot.sh` renders the real views into its own window with `cacheDisplay`, because the terminal can't capture other windows. Look at every image after a UI change. A window that comes out enormous means a view reports a huge minimum size, usually text with `.fixedSize(horizontal: false, vertical: true)` and no definite width. Given an architecture file, it renders that map fitted, zoomed out, with a component selected, on a walkthrough step, in Flows by actor and by area, in Data, on an explainer slide and in each theme. To check a problem seen at another zoom, give a `DemoScene` zoom steps with `zoomOut` (negative zooms in).
- The layout must place every card without overlaps for any graph: cycles, self-connections, empty groups, nodes without a group and entities without a store included. After changing `Layout.swift`, `FlowDiagram.swift` or `DataLayout`, run `scripts/fuzz-layout.sh`, then look at the screenshots. The demo architectures are small, so also check a real map with 20 or more components: export one with `blueprint show <app> --json > /tmp/app.json` and pass it to `scripts/screenshot.sh`. Don't commit other apps' architectures.
- The words are half the product: people open an app to learn how it works. Keep the inspector scannable, with the role in large type first and short points after it, and keep `blueprint guide` asking agents for a walkthrough, roles and details written for a newcomer.
- On the canvas, attach gestures and `.onHover` to a card before `.position`: after it, they cover the whole diagram and the last card takes every click. Don't put `.help` tooltips on canvas views either, since they take clicks and hover away from SwiftUI's gestures.
- Screenshots can't show whether clicks work. To test clicks, post mouse events to the harness window with `NSApp.postEvent`. The harness can't become the active app, so a SwiftUI tap gesture ignores that first click unless the view under it returns true from `acceptsFirstMouse(for:)` (buttons always do). Override it on the hit view's class for the test, or the test fails for reasons the real app doesn't have.
- Themes restyle the canvases, never the layout: every theme draws the same frames and routes. A view on the canvas reads `diagramTheme` from the environment and keeps its Blueprint look as is, with the other themes going through `DiagramStyle`. Terminal and ASCII draw routes with right angles from `Route.corners`. Terminal is always dark, so `ZoomableCanvas` sets that appearance on what it draws. ASCII, Minimal and Blueprint follow the app's appearance. Explainers and the welcome demo stay in Blueprint whatever the theme. A new view on a canvas needs all four themes, and the screenshot harness has a scene for each.
- Explainers are played by Blueprint, never rendered by agents. Agents write scenes that point at ids, and the look lives in `ExplainerPlayer.swift`, so every app's explainers match. Keep the stage light in every appearance, and keep sound to effects tied to what happens on screen, with nothing playing under them.
- Hovering a card only highlights its connections. Only a selection dims the rest, because the pointer crosses cards all the time and a diagram that fades on every hover looks broken.
- The copy in daily use is `/Applications/Blueprint.app`, with `/usr/local/bin/blueprint` linked to its bundled command. After a change, run `scripts/build-release.sh`, quit the app, replace the installed copy with `ditto` and open it again. Do it as part of the change, not as a step left for later: an old copy also means an old `blueprint` command, which silently drops the parts of the format it doesn't know when agents save. Unset `BLUEPRINT_HOME` first, or a test value left in the shell opens fake data.
- The agent skill is `skill/blueprint/SKILL.md`, and it ships inside the app. Install Agent Skill, or step 2 of the setup guide, copies it to `~/.agents/skills/blueprint`, and the app refreshes that copy at launch when it's out of date, so an update brings agents the skill that matches its command.
- Every release follows the updater's contract: a public repo, the `vX.Y.Z` tag equal to `MARKETING_VERSION`, and the zip from `scripts/build-release.sh` attached to the release. The update dialog shows the release notes up to their `## Install` heading, so what's new goes first.
- Edits made by tools sometimes reach the disk a moment after they return, and `swiftc` then fails with "was modified during the build". Wait a second and run it again.
- The app isn't sandboxed, because it reads folders across the home folder. Releases are signed with Flavio Copes's Developer ID and notarized by `scripts/build-release.sh`. Without that certificate, on CI or in a fork, the same script keeps Xcode's ad-hoc signature, so the build still works.
